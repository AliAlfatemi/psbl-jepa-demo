4 · How it works

A JEPA can minimise its prediction loss with a constant encoder, so it needs an anti-collapse term. PSBL (Predictive-Sufficient Bi-Lipschitz) asks pairwise embedding distances to stay inside a band around a reference distance: a cross-fitted ridge estimate of how different two samples' predicted futures are, computed once from raw training data without labels. The lower edge penalizes pairwise collapse whenever the fixed reference separates the samples; the upper edge penalizes variation not supported by the fixed predictive reference. In our controlled benchmark this recovered the true factors while ignoring nuisance; on active_matter it trains stably but does not clearly separate from VICReg on the held-out test split. The subspace criteria PGRF and NPMR were rejected diagnostic candidates, and empirical SIGReg statements in the paper apply only to the repository SIGReg implementation.
